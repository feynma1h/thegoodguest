0 The whole app in one map — a front door and a camera, nothing more

The iOS app is deliberately narrow: it establishes identity, captures a room beautifully, and carries the file to the web — where the reveal, the guest, and the redesign live. The design job is to make a small surface feel intentional and premium, and to make the handoff read as a doorway, never a dead end. Good Guest language, translated to iOS idioms: SF Symbols, native navigation & sheets, safe areas, system haptics, standard gestures.

MY POSITION — HEADLESS vs LIVE AR
Capture should be live AR-guided, LiDAR-first — but quiet.
The phone's one job is producing a good capture. A headless screen hides the one thing that matters most — whether you're actually getting the room. "Did I get enough?" is the core anxiety, and you can't answer it honestly while blind. RoomPlan/LiDAR already yields a real-time mesh; showing it turns an anxious blind walk into a legible one.
But not a neon-wireframe game. Restraint is the brand: no camera photo-feed as hero (Good Guest generates no photos), no coverage percentage. Instead the LiDAR mesh paints as ink-on-parchment, surfaces filling in like a sketch being completed. Coverage is felt — the sketch settling — not scored.
THE TIER SPLIT DOES DOUBLE DUTY
The non-LiDAR (ARKIT_ONLY) fallback isn't a lesser copy of the same screen — it's the headless treatment, honestly. Where geometry can't be trusted, we don't fake a mesh: we show tracking quality + a coverage ring + guidance, and say so plainly. The premium pipeline is LiDAR; the graceful degrade is the minimal view. One decision resolves both the open question and the tier strategy. See §3 →

REVIEW · CONFIRM BEFORE UPLOAD
The finished sketch shown whole, with mono capture metrics (the one place raw numbers belong). "Add a little more" returns to capture with progress intact. "Send it home" begins the background upload (§5).
"DID I GET ENOUGH?" — ANSWERED THREE WAYS
During capture the sketch fills (felt). At enough, the guest says so (spoken). At review, the capture is shown whole with a plain verdict (seen). The user never has to guess — and if coverage is thin, the review copy shifts to "I've got the bones, but a few gaps. Worth another minute?" with "Add more" as the primary.
NO PREVIEW, ON PURPOSE
The review shows the sketch, never a rendered 3D room. The real room belongs to the reveal, on the web. Showing a rough version here would spend the magic early and set the wrong expectation for phone-side fidelity.
5 Upload & processing — honest waiting, narrated as arrival

Files are large; upload runs in the background and survives app close via a Live Activity. Once uploaded, the copy narrates analysis — the room being understood — never pipeline internals. A server-anchored clock keeps time honest across relaunches. Every waiting state has a felt temperature: patient, then reassuring, then candid if it runs long.

WHY NOT REVEAL ON THE PHONE?
The reveal is a cinematic, room-scale experience the web owns. Cramming it onto the phone would both duplicate the web's crown jewel and diminish it. The doorway reframes "go elsewhere" as anticipation — the room is somewhere better, and you're being invited in.
CONTINUITY, GUARANTEED
Because identity is shared (§8), the desk knows it's you the instant you arrive — no re-login, no "sync." The web greets you mid-thought, exactly as its own landing spec describes the returning state.

7 When it goes wrong — honest words, a clear next step, and a banner that waits for you

Failure follows the web system's rule: the guest owns what it couldn't do, never blames the user, and always offers exactly one concrete path. Two kinds — recoverable (part of the room made it) and terminal (nothing survived) — plus an upload-failed banner that resurfaces on relaunch so a failure is never silently lost.

9 Continuity with the web — notifications, the QR bridge, and just enough room history

My call on history: the phone keeps a thin recent-rooms list — status and a way back to the web, nothing editable. Room management (renaming, comparing, deleting) is conversational and belongs to the guest on the web. The phone is a camera with a memory of what it's sent, not a library. It also receives the web's knocks (push) and can accept a scan session handed back from the desk (QR / deep link).
