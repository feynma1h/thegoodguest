3The Wait — the guest is on their way, and the conversation starts before the room exists

Minutes, honestly owned. The guest gathers context that will shape the first analysis — wait time is when the relationship starts. Progress is narrated as arrival, never as pipeline. The reveal never fires into an empty tab.

THE HOLD RULE
The reveal never auto-plays. If reconstruction finishes while you're away, the room waits behind a door: a still, darkened threshold and one line — “It's ready. Come in when you have a minute — this is worth your full attention.”
HONESTY ABOUT TIME

4The Reveal — threshold, permission, first words; degrades objects-first

As locked in 8a/8d: camera arrives through the doorway, stops, asks. The first words are specific admiration informed by what you said during the wait. When early builds produce furniture before walls, the choreography degrades gracefully — assembly becomes the show.

When a build ships furniture without architecture, the reveal becomes an assembly: the doorway framing is skipped; objects arrive one at a time onto the bare ground plane, each named as it lands — sofa, then table, then the lamp — and the guest treats it as moving-day, not failure:
